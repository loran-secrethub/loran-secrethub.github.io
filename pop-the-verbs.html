Pop the Verbs

An underwater bubble-popping game where players find the verbs hiding among other words, then review what they missed.

The idea

Why a game?

Spotting verbs on a worksheet is quiet and slow. In this game, every pop is a quick decision, and a sound and a little animation tell the player right away whether they got it right.

And the round doesn't end with just a score. A review screen shows every mistake and every missed verb, so each round becomes a learning moment.

Every pop asks

Is this a verb?

How it plays

Learn, pop, score, review

  1. 1Learn

    A kid-friendly How to Play card explains that verbs are action words, with little bobbing bubbles showing which words to pop and which to leave alone.

  2. 2Pop

    30 word bubbles float around an underwater scene: 15 verbs and 15 other words. Players get 15 pops. A right pop turns green and bursts. A wrong pop turns red and shakes.

  3. 3Score

    After 15 pops, a results card bounces in with the score, up to three stars and a cheering message.

  4. 4Review

    Let's Review shows two lists: words popped by mistake, and verbs that were missed. Then players can play again with fresh words.

Under the hood

How I built it

The game is split into four screens, each with one job:

  1. Bootloads the font
  2. Preloaderpictures, sounds, loading bar
  3. Main menumusic, tutorial
  4. Gameplay, score, review

  • Bubbles that bounce

    Each bubble floats in a random direction and bounces off the edges. Its word follows it many times a second, so they always travel together.

  • Pops that feel real

    A right pop grows and fades like a bursting bubble. A wrong pop shakes first. One shared piece of code handles both.

  • Remembering mistakes

    Every wrong pop is saved to a list. When the round ends, the game also checks which verbs are still floating. Both lists feed the review screen.

  • A moving sea

    The game can't play GIFs by itself, so the swimming-fish GIF sits behind a see-through game screen. It fills any screen, with no black bars.

  • One mute button

    Custom sound on and off icons, built once and shared by the menu and the game. It remembers the player's choice between screens.

  • Clean pops

    A popped bubble stops moving and can't be clicked twice. Once its animation ends, it's removed, so the game stays smooth.
