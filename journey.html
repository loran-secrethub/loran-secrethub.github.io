Journey

From the classroom to the code behind the course

About six years in education, then into building. Here's how one led to the other.

Five chapters

  1. 1

    ESL Teacher · About 1 year

    Every learner is different

    I started by teaching English to foreign students, from preschoolers to teenagers. Each age group needed a different approach, and adapting to very different learners became the first skill I relied on.

    What I carried forward

    Start with who the learner is, not with the content.

  2. 2

    Preschool & ICT Lead Teacher · About 4 years

    Teaching through a screen

    I started with Nursery, then the pandemic moved my Kindergarten class entirely onto Zoom. I taught myself Google Classroom and Koobits to keep lessons running.

    Later, teaching ICT to grade schoolers, I introduced coding through Scratch and other free block-based platforms. Keeping them free mattered to me. Before long, I was the person colleagues asked when technology got in the way, so I started helping and training other teachers.

    What I carried forward

    Make tools feel less scary for the people using them.

  3. 3

    Robotics Lead Teacher · About 6 months

    Robots on the classroom floor

    For about six months I taught robotics with construction kits and battery-powered robots that students programmed through the Rocomo app. The role was short, but it's where coding stopped being a screen-only activity for me.

    What I carried forward

    Code clicks when you can see it move.

  4. 4

    Articulate Storyline Developer

    From teaching lessons to building them

    I moved from delivering lessons to building them. In Articulate Storyline I turned lesson content into interactive digital lessons using variables, triggers and layers, and packaged them so platforms could track each learner's progress.

    What I carried forward

    Learning doesn't have to feel like a chore.

  5. 5

    Full Stack E-learning Developer · Now

    Building the tools

    Today I write code to solve real problems for my team. I build browser tools that make reviewing easier, scripts that save hours of repetitive work, and tracking that shows how learners move through a lesson. And when a lesson needs to feel like play, I build games.

    Still a teacher

    I plan tools the way I used to plan lessons: starting from what people struggle with.
